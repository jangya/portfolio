I was fourteen when I got my first digital phone. There was a Snake game on it, and I couldn’t stop wondering how that single pixel moved across a tiny screen, who wrote the rules, and whether I could write them too.

That itch never left. In college, while my classmates attended lectures, I spent nights teaching myself HTML and JavaScript, building terrible websites I was inexplicably proud of. Those sleepless nights—fueled by curiosity and instant noodles—became the foundation of everything I do today.

## The Real Story Was Abstraction

My first serious professional chapter was at **Adept Concept Private Limited**, a startup where I worked with jQuery, PHP, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It wasn’t glamorous, but it was foundational. I was close to the browser, close to the DOM, close to the request-response cycle, and close to every mistake I made.

<PullQuote>
  Abstractions are useful only when you understand what they are hiding.
</PullQuote>

Looking back, the past decade wasn’t really a story about moving from jQuery to Angular to React. It was a story about abstraction:

As the web matured, my work moved into larger systems where frontend stopped sitting at the edge and became part of the core architecture.

  The moment we moved complex business logic to the browser, frontend stopped

Understanding how a UI talks to services, how containers isolate build artifacts, and how the cloud amplifies both reach and failure surfaces isn’t purely a backend concern anymore. It’s a frontend concern too.

## The Last Pillar

Today, as a Staff Software Engineer at **Edelman Financial Engines**, I lead frontend architecture and GenAI integration at scale. The stack has evolved to React, Zustand, and AI-powered advisory platforms, but the central question hasn’t changed:

<PullQuote>
  How do you build a UI that is fast, accessible, and honest about what it is
  doing?
</PullQuote>

I believe UI is the last pillar holding any application upright. Backend services can be swapped. Databases can be migrated. AI models will be iterated on monthly. But the interface between human intention and digital capability is permanent.

No amount of AI code generation removes the need to decide what a user sees first, how errors are communicated, when to delegate to a model, and when to keep a human in the loop. Those are architectural decisions—they require judgment, taste, and the lived experience of knowing what breaks in production.

That conviction led me to pursue an **M.Tech in AI/ML at BITS Pilani**—not to chase hype, but to understand these systems from the inside out and write about where AI and frontend architecture actually intersect.

AI can generate code, but it cannot rescue a weak mental model. If you don’t understand how the browser works, how state flows, why accessibility fails, or where performance breaks, you can’t debug what AI hands you.

<PullQuote>If you cannot build it, you cannot truly debug it.</PullQuote>

The most interesting work in our field isn’t about what AI can generate. It’s about what it cannot replace.
